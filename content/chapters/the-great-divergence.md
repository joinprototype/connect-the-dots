summary: Qianlong’s refusal, Lin Zexu’s attempt to suppress opium, Britain’s parliamentary fight and the Treaty of Nanking. An investigation of commercial power, coercion and the disputed causes of the Great Divergence.
---

In 1793 Qianlong told Britain’s king that China had no use for his manufactures. Forty-nine years later, British and Qing negotiators signed a treaty opening five ports, ceding Hong Kong and requiring China to pay $21 million.

Between those documents lay an opium trade, a commissioner’s demand to stop it and a British argument over whether the treatment of merchants justified force. Their decisions explain how Britain imposed these terms. The larger explanations of its economic lead come after the treaty.
