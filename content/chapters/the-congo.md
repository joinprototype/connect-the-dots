title: The Red Rubber
subtitle: What was done to the people of the Congo under Leopold II's rule, who said so, who answered, and how Belgium has faced it
summary: From 1885 to 1908 the Congo Free State was ruled by Leopold II as its sovereign, apart from Belgium. Villagers, a British consul and the State's own commission described forced rubber, hostages, killings and cut-off hands. Here is the record, with each finding and each answer kept apart.
---

From 1885 to 1908 the Congo Free State was ruled by King Leopold II as its sovereign, not by Belgium; Belgium took it over on 15 November 1908. Rubber was gathered by the State's own agents and soldiers and by concession companies in which the State held shares. Belgians administered it, and most staff and practices stayed after 1908.

Villagers fled, hid from soldiers, or carried the names of their dead to an inquiry. Roger Casement recorded their testimony; the Free State challenged his findings, then its own commissioners confirmed killings, hostages and mutilations. Many witnesses' names were withheld in print and replaced with letters. Nobody has an exact count of the dead. More than a century later, the Belgian Chamber's experts would return to the testimony and to the question of responsibility.
