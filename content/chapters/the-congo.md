title: The Red Rubber
subtitle: What was done to the people of the Congo under Leopold II's rule, who said so, who answered, and how Belgium has faced it
summary: From 1885 to 1908 the Congo Free State was ruled by Leopold II as its sovereign, apart from Belgium. Villagers, a British consul and the State's own commission described forced rubber, hostages, killings and cut-off hands. Here is the record, with each finding and each answer kept apart.
---

From 1885 to 1908 the Congo Free State was ruled by King Leopold II as its sovereign, not by Belgium; Belgium took it over on 15 November 1908. Rubber was gathered by the State's own agents and soldiers and by concession companies in which the State held shares. Belgians administered it, and most staff and practices stayed after 1908.

This story keeps four things apart: what the record shows, what a named body found, what the State and its defenders said in answer, and what is only alleged. The British consul Roger Casement, the Free State's own Commission of Inquiry, the campaigners and the Belgian Chamber's experts of 2024 found different things, and each is given in its own words. Congolese villagers speak where the record holds their words, often as letters, because the printed names were withheld. Nobody has an exact count of the dead; the later scenes give the ranges and who made them. Some sources are in French; the narration translates them. There are no pictures: the story tells what happened without showing it.

  narration: "**How many.** No one has an exact count. The highest figure is ten million deaths, which the Belgian experts trace to Adam Hochschild (1998); another reading, from authors the experts do not name, is no net fall given migration. The experts find it impossible to deny that deaths rose, from violence, fewer births and disease among people driven from their land. The demographer Jean-Paul Sanderson works back from 10 million people in 1935, using surveys and administrative counts. He calls 20 million in 1885 highly improbable, 15 million far likelier, 11.5 million conceivable. By subtraction that is a fall of 1.5 to 5 million people over fifty years, 27 of them under Belgium: a fall, not a death toll. The 1905 Commission wrote of “the absence of a census” and said that, if Stanley is accepted, “a large part of the population has disappeared.” The experts say the lack of exact figures casts no doubt on the decline nor on the massive scale of the violence. On genocide they find intent cannot be shown, since the rulers needed labour, and warn that the word must not hide the regime's inherently violent nature."
