summary: In 1781 the crew of the slave ship Zong threw captive people into the sea, and in 1783 the owners sued their insurers for the price. Here are the policies, loans and compensation around the trade, and what Lloyd's, Aetna, JPMorgan Chase, Brown, Georgetown and Harvard have each said, from court reports, the institutions' own words and the historians who counted.
---

In 1781 the crew of the Zong threw captive people into the sea. In 1783 the ship's owners sued their insurers for the price.

Most lines here come from a court report, an institution's own statement or report, a government record, or a historian who read the ledgers; several rest on press copies of statements (Associated Press, BBC, Reuters), because the originals could not be opened, and the story says so where it matters. JPMorgan's letter, Georgetown's full report and University College London's records were not read directly. Where counts differ, the story gives the ranges it found and says who counted. An allegation is told as one, with who made it. A bank or a college of today is not the same as a firm of 1800 unless a source traces the line, and the story says how, and by whose account. Brown and Harvard did not apologise, and the story says what each did say.
