subtitle: The Zong killings, the price the owners claimed, and the demand to answer for them
summary: In 1781 the crew of the Zong threw living people into the sea. The owners sought insurance money; Olaudah Equiano brought the news to Granville Sharp, who sought prosecutions. The insurance verdict was reopened, nobody was prosecuted, and the case became a weapon for abolitionists. Lloyd’s later apology and opened ledgers return the question to the trade it insured.
---

The crew threw living people into the sea. The owners asked for £30 a person. Equiano and Sharp demanded justice for the people killed.

The Zong’s voyage, the insurance claim and the attempt to secure prosecutions lead from a captive’s plea to the words of the judges, the campaigners and the insurance market that has since answered for its role in the trade.

  narration: "**For the blood.** The new-trial order did not satisfy Sharp. In his 1808 History, Clarkson recalled Sharp making the facts public and sending them with the trial to the Admiralty; he also recalled an earlier letter against slavery being published as knowledge of the Zong spread. The campaigners carried the case beyond the insurance dispute. Abolition would bring another decision about who should receive money."
