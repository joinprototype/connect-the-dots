summary: Epstein had clients for financial work, and banks that held his money. Here is what courts, regulators, universities and the people themselves have put on the record, and what still is not known.
---

Jeffrey Epstein pleaded guilty in Florida in 2008 to two state charges, felony solicitation of prostitution and procurement of minors to engage in prostitution. In 2019 he was indicted on federal charges of sex trafficking of minors, and he died that year in a New York jail before trial. The Justice Department and FBI memo of July 2025 says its review “confirmed that Epstein harmed over one thousand victims. Each suffered unique trauma.” The same memo says the review “revealed no incriminating ‘client list.’” That is the government's answer to a popular belief. This story asks a plainer question: who paid Epstein for financial work, who banked him, who took his gifts, and what does each say?

Every sum is the one a court, regulator, university or person gave, and the story says who counted. A dealing on the record shows that two parties met, paid or banked. By itself it shows neither knowledge of a crime nor a part in one. Most of this is not a court finding: a settlement decides nothing, and a senator's report is one senator's. Each person and body named gets their reply where one was found, and the story says where none was. Several documents were read only through reports of them: Dechert's review for Apollo, Senator Wyden's report, Goodwin Procter's report for MIT, Harvard's report and the House transcripts. The New York order on Deutsche Bank was read in part. Read up to 2 November 2026. The case of 2008 is in [Palm Beach](/c/the-palm-beach-case/), the fight over the files in [The Address Book](/c/the-address-book/), and the claim that he worked for a spy agency in [Was He a Spy?](/c/was-he-a-spy/).
