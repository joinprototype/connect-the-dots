summary: Between 1898 and 1947, U.S. presidents, Congress and private banks acquired territory, financed wars and recovery, and helped write monetary rules. Aguinaldo, Mabini, Colombia, European recovery governments and Molotov give their answers beside the U.S. decisions.
---

In 1898 the United States defeated Spain and claimed the Philippines. By 1947 its government was a creditor to former great powers, the dollar stood at the center of negotiated monetary rules, and Washington could offer to finance Europe’s recovery.

The scale changed through decisions: overseas occupation, more than $10 billion in government war loans, about $50 billion in Lend-Lease, and institutions agreed by 44 governments. The dollars are those of the time. Power met resistance as well as consent: Aguinaldo and Mabini demanded independence; Colombia disputed Panama’s separation; Molotov rejected the recovery framework.
