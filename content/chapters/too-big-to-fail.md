summary: "A test of the claim that nothing changed after Standard Oil’s 1911 breakup and that U.S. markets are now monopolies. From Rockefeller’s share and the 1929 stockholder fight to the bank rescue that gave the phrase its name and the 2023–2026 cases, with who counted, the court’s finding, each firm’s reply and the economists’ dispute."
---

A common version of this story says three things: that Standard Oil’s 1911 breakup into 34 companies changed nothing, because Rockefeller stayed the richest man in the world and the 34 stayed his pawns; that U.S. markets are now monopolies whatever their companies say; and that buyers therefore have no real choice. It is an old version: Time wrote in 1923 of “the Standard Oil monopoly, for practical monopoly it still is,” and the ABA Journal says Theodore Roosevelt, after the breakup, quoted Wall Street’s prayer: “give us another dissolution.”

This story tests that version rather than illustrating it. It sets out, market by market, who counted, what a court or regulator found, and what each company said in reply. A market share is not proof of harm, a settlement is not a finding of guilt, and a link between two firms is not evidence of wrongdoing. Cases are read to 2 November 2026; where a later step exists and was not read, the card says so.
