summary: "A test of the claim that nothing changed after Standard Oil’s 1911 breakup and that U.S. markets are now monopolies. From Rockefeller’s share, the FTC’s 1917 finding and the 1929 stockholder fight to the 1984 bank hearing where the phrase was used and the 2023–2026 cases, with who counted, the court’s finding, each firm’s reply and the economists’ dispute."
---

A common version of this story says three things: that Standard Oil’s 1911 breakup into 34 companies changed nothing, because Rockefeller stayed the richest man in the world and the 34 stayed his pawns; that U.S. markets are now monopolies whatever their companies say; and that buyers therefore have no real choice. It is an old version: Time wrote in 1923 of “the Standard Oil monopoly, for practical monopoly it still is,” and the ABA Journal says Theodore Roosevelt, after the breakup, quoted Wall Street’s prayer: “give us another dissolution.”

By 2 November 2026, courts had found monopolies in Google markets and a jury had found monopolization by Live Nation, while Meta had defeated the FTC and Apple had beaten Epic’s federal monopoly claims. Choices varied sharply by place: FCC staff counted two or more wired broadband providers for 62% of urban households but only 23% of rural ones. The question is what officials did when ownership survived a breakup, a bank faced a run or buyers had nowhere else to turn.
