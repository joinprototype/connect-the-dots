title: Signed Off
subtitle: Who checks the books, and what happened when one of the five big checkers was charged
summary: Arthur Andersen reminded its Enron team about document retention while an SEC investigation approached. Employees shredded records; a jury convicted the firm of obstruction in June 2002. The Supreme Court reversed the conviction in May 2005 because the jury instructions were wrong. By then Andersen had stopped auditing public companies, and most of its clients had gone to the four firms left.
---

An outside auditor seeks reasonable assurance against material error or fraud and signs an opinion on a company's accounts. At Enron, that auditor was Arthur Andersen. When investigators wanted the working papers, employees had destroyed many of them. The criminal case that followed ended Andersen's public-company audit practice before the Supreme Court reversed its conviction.
