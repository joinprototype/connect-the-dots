title: Signed Off
subtitle: Who hires and pays the auditor, and what regulators and courts said when audits were challenged
summary: Companies pay the firms that audit them, and four firms audit nearly all the largest. Here is the record from the Big Eight to the Big Four, from Enron and the Supreme Court to Lehman, Wirecard, Carillion and Evergrande, with each firm’s own reply and what each case did and did not decide.
---

Four firms audit nearly every large listed company, and the companies pay them. Here is what regulators, courts and the firms themselves said when their audits were challenged.

Most lines here come from court opinions, regulators’ orders, government reports and the firms’ own statements, many of them as reported in the press. These are the cases that reached a regulator or a court, not a sample of audits. A charge is an accusation, not a finding of guilt: where a case ended, the story says how. A regulator’s finding is that regulator’s finding, and each firm’s reply stands beside it. The story does not decide who was right, and none of the regulators or courts quoted here found that a firm signed accounts because it was paid.
