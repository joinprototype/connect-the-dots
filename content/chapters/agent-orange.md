title: Rainbow Herbicides
subtitle: Who made Agent Orange, what they and the government knew, and what the courts did and did not decide
summary: From 1962 to 1971 the U.S. sprayed Vietnam with herbicides, Agent Orange the best known. Seven companies made it. Here is the road from the 1961 decision to the cleanup of two air bases, from court records, official reports and the words of veterans and Vietnamese plaintiffs.
---

Between 1962 and 1971 the U.S. Air Force sprayed about 19 million gallons of herbicides over South Vietnam, 11 to 12 million of them Agent Orange. Seven U.S. companies made over 99% of the Orange, and it carried a dioxin. In 1984 they paid $180 million to a class of Vietnam veterans and families, denying any liability. Vietnamese plaintiffs sued in 2004; courts dismissed their suit on the law, and the Supreme Court declined review in 2009. Since 1991 U.S. law has presumed some illnesses in Vietnam veterans to be service-connected, and since 2012 the U.S. has paid to clean two Vietnamese air bases, while denying any legal duty to do so. What Agent Orange caused, and who owes what, is still argued.

Nearly every line here comes from court opinions, the Air Force's own history, Congress's research service, and the words of veterans and Vietnamese people as courts, officials and reporters recorded them. Where counts differ, the story says who counted. The makers and the government each get their own say. A court's reading of the evidence, a settlement and an allegation are told as what they are, not as findings of guilt.
