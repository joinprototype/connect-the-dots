  narration: "**What one march could do.** In 2021 Martina Topić looked at 294 *Times* articles on women and smoking or smoking and health from 1870 to 1929, tracing the debate from well before the parade. She argues the march joined an existing campaign against a double standard and reads the parade item as coverage Bernays earned, with little basis there for claiming a major effect from the march alone. Bernays got a scene staged for a cigarette maker into newspapers, and his memoir helped make it a model for public relations. The coverage shows how the message traveled, and the evidence Topić and Murphree examine stops short of showing that the event changed women’s smoking habits or Lucky Strike sales."
