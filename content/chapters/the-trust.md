subtitle: How one Cleveland refiner came to control American oil, why the country broke it up, and what became of the pieces
summary: In 1870 Standard Oil was one refiner among many in Cleveland. Within years it led the country's oil, and in 1911 the Supreme Court ordered it broken up. Here is the road between, from court records, sworn testimony, and the words of those for and against it.
---

In 1870 Standard Oil was one refiner among many in Cleveland. By 1906, the Bureau of Corporations put its share of American illuminating oil at 80 to 90 per cent. In 1911 the Supreme Court upheld its breakup for unlawful restraint and monopolization. The Briscoe Center counts 34 separate companies after dissolution; the decree itself covered the New Jersey parent and 37 subsidiaries.

A refiner sold because he said he faced ruin. Producers pledged to withhold their oil. Rockefeller defended his company as a way to cut waste and prices; judges ruled that it had broken the law. Rebates continued after the South Improvement Company failed, including payments tied to rivals’ shipments. The separated businesses later supplied the roots of Exxon, Mobil, Chevron, Amoco, Marathon and others.
