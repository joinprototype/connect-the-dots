subtitle: How one Cleveland refiner came to control American oil, why the country broke it up, and what became of the pieces
summary: In 1870 Standard Oil was one refiner among many in Cleveland. Within years it led the country's oil, and in 1911 the Supreme Court ordered it broken up. Here is the road between, from court records, sworn testimony, and the words of those for and against it.
---

In 1870 Standard Oil was one refiner among many in Cleveland. In 1911 the Supreme Court ordered it broken up.

A refiner sold because he said he faced ruin. Producers pledged to withhold their oil. Rockefeller defended his company as a way to cut waste and prices; judges ruled that it had broken the law. By 1906, the Bureau of Corporations put its share of American illuminating oil at 80 to 90 per cent.

  narration: "**White orders a breakup.** In 1911 Chief Justice Edward White faced twenty-three volumes—about 12,000 pages—of testimony about Standard Oil and upheld the order to dissolve it. He allowed at least six months instead of thirty days to obey because of the business’s size and complexity, and eased an injunction on interstate oil commerce to prevent serious injury to the public. Standard had to separate its companies; White's reasoning would also govern how other combinations were judged."
