---

In 1600 a group of London merchants received a royal charter. By 1858 their company had collected the taxes of Bengal, sold opium that came to be fought over in China, been examined by Parliament, and lost its government of India to the Crown.

A charter gave merchants a monopoly; military victories gave them rulers to bargain with; an emperor's grant gave them the revenue of Bengal. The people paying those taxes, the officials collecting them and the men defending their conduct in London left sharply different explanations of what followed.
