title: User Fees
subtitle: "How money and people move between drug makers and their regulators: what courts found, what the law allows, and what is only suspected"
summary: "In 1992 Congress let the FDA charge drug makers fees to speed its reviews, and they now pay about three-quarters of the drug-review program. This story sets that beside lobbying filings, job moves, a 1989 case in which FDA staff pleaded guilty to taking gratuities (which involved no fees), and four drug cases, keeping each kind of evidence apart."
---

A drug company needs an agency’s yes before it can sell a pill. This story follows the money and the people around that yes, and it keeps four kinds of evidence apart.

**A court’s verdict** is a conviction or plea, and only then does the story use the court’s own words for the crime. **An official body’s finding** is a congressional finding, an agency order or a committee report, told as that body’s. **Legal money and job moves** are fees, lobbying and moves between jobs that the law allows and reports; they show what was paid and who went where, not that any decision was bought. **Suspicion** is what someone alleges or fears, told as theirs, with their name on it.

Most lines come from agency and Justice Department records of court cases, lobbying filings, congressional reports, reporters and the people involved. Where counts differ, the story says who counted.
