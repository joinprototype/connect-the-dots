title: The Ransom
subtitle: How Spaniards took two rulers of the Americas, what became of the gold and silver, and what has been asked of Spain since
summary: In 1519 Cortés took Moctezuma; in 1532 Pizarro took Atahualpa. Here are both seizures told by the conquerors and by the conquered, where the gold and silver went, and the apology asked of Spain in 2019.
---

In November 1519 Hernán Cortés entered Tenochtitlan. In November 1532 Francisco Pizarro took the Inca ruler Atahualpa at Cajamarca. Within a year of each capture the ruler was dead, and the Crown of Castile had claimed its fifth of what was taken, though much of Cortés’s first gold was lost on the retreat.

The words here are those of Cortés, of Bernal Díaz, who marched with him and wrote in old age, of Pizarro’s secretary and his cousin, and of Nahua and Andean writers who put down the story decades later: the Florentine Codex under a Franciscan friar, Salcamayhua about 1620, Guaman Poma about 1615. Each is named when it speaks, and where the accounts disagree the story gives each its say and does not pick one. The popular tale that the Aztecs took Cortés for a god is told with its history.

Not read: the account of Titu Cusi, Betanzos, and anything written by a Tlaxcalan or Texcocan. Matthew Restall is quoted through a lesson sheet, and Melissa Dell through an abstract; the books and article behind them were not read.
