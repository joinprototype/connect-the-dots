title: The Ransom
subtitle: Atahualpa’s bargain and the Crown’s silver revenues
summary: At Cajamarca, Atahualpa offered gold and silver for his life. His captors divided the metal and killed him. Later, the Crown drew recurring revenues from mines such as Potosí.
---

At Cajamarca in 1532, Francisco Pizarro took Atahualpa captive. Atahualpa offered gold and silver, his captors divided the metal, and the governor put him to death. Some of that treasure reached Charles V. Later mines gave the Crown a recurring source of silver revenues, which Philip II used to service some of his debts.
