---

In 1982 a Milan bank collapsed and its chairman was found hanging beneath a London bridge. Two years later the Vatican, through its bank, agreed to pay the bank’s creditors $244 to $250 million and said it owed nothing in law.

Where a court’s own text was out of reach, this story relies on press accounts of the inquests, the parliamentary report on P2, the Vatican’s own statements and established newspapers, and says so. It says which court, which body and which date. A settlement is not a ruling, a warrant is not a verdict, and an acquittal is an acquittal. The Vatican’s bank, the Holy See and individual clergy are kept apart. A connection is not evidence of wrongdoing.
