In 1982 a Milan bank collapsed and its chairman was found hanging beneath a London bridge. Two years later the Vatican, through its bank, agreed to pay the bank’s creditors $244 to $250 million and said it owed nothing in law.

A press report on Casaroli’s papers, hosted by the Archdiocese of Milan, describes the disagreement behind the payment: Secretary of State Agostino Casaroli chose compensation; Archbishop Paul Marcinkus, who ran the bank, resisted. Cardinal Carlo Maria Martini’s private letter, quoted by that report, asked why Marcinkus still held office while people had lost their savings. In London and Rome, Calvi’s family pressed for an answer about his death.
