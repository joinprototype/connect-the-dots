summary: The U.S. fired 67 nuclear weapons in the Marshall Islands, the Soviet Union 456 in Kazakhstan. Here are the islanders and villagers in their own words where the record has them, the records of both governments, and what each government answered.
---

Between 1946 and 1958 the United States fired 67 nuclear weapons in the Marshall Islands. Between 1949 and 1989 the Soviet Union fired 456 at Semipalatinsk, in Kazakhstan. People lived beside both sites.

On 1 March 1954 the biggest U.S. test, Bravo, put fallout on Marshallese atolls, 28 U.S. servicemen and 23 Japanese fishermen. The Atomic Energy Commission’s chairman counted 236 Marshallese moved; the 401 people of Ailuk were not moved; the National Cancer Institute counts 14,000 residents exposed to the whole testing program. A Marshall Islands tribunal set up under the 1986 agreement awarded over $87 million for personal injuries and had paid $71.7 million by the end of 2004. The Congressional Research Service and Enewetak’s counsel David Cohen report land awards totaling about $2.3 billion for four atolls, almost all unpaid; Cohen puts the unpaid total at over $3 billion in 2021 dollars. In Kazakhstan the Soviet military ran the site in secrecy, and the Kazakh republic’s president closed it by decree on 29 August 1991. Togzhan Kassenova reports 220,000 affected; Kazakhstan’s embassy in Finland says more than 1.3 million exposed; a state institute says more than 1.5 million suffered.

The islanders petitioned the United Nations, sued for their land and left islands they had been assured were safe. Near Semipalatinsk, doctors recorded injuries the military denied, and protesters demanded an end to testing. Both governments defended their programs; survivors demanded treatment, compensation and a home they could trust. For the bombs themselves see [[the-most-terrible-bomb]].
