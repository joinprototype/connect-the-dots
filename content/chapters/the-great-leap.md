subtitle: "Famine, political campaigns, 4 June 1989 and Xinjiang under the People's Republic of China, each told from its own evidence"
summary: "The Great Leap Forward famine, the campaigns, the Cultural Revolution, Tibet in 1959, Tiananmen and Xinjiang: what each source shows, what a named body found, what is only alleged, and what China says in its own words."
---

Estimates of deaths in the Great Leap Forward famine range from 18 to at least 45 million. Yang Jisheng's and Frank Dikötter's totals include violence as well as starvation; their methods and the reasons for the famine remain disputed. The Party's own 1981 history acknowledges serious losses and errors by Mao and other leaders.

These are different events, not one tally: the famine, the political campaigns, Tibet in 1959, Tiananmen and Xinjiang. For Xinjiang, the findings concern detention, birth control and labour. Government determinations and parliamentary motions calling it genocide stand alongside the UN human rights office's finding that detention may constitute crimes against humanity, and China's rejection of the charges.
