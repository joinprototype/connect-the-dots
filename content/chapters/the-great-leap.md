summary: "The Great Leap Forward famine, the campaigns, the Cultural Revolution, Tibet in 1959, Tiananmen and Xinjiang: what each source shows, what a named body found, what is only alleged, and what China says in its own words."
---

This story covers the hardest years of the People's Republic. These are different events, not one tally: the famine and the campaigns killed people, while for Xinjiang the sources here describe detention, birth control and labour, not deaths. That people starved in 1959 to 1961 is not what the sources dispute. How many, why, and who answers for it, they do.

Neither Tombstone nor Mao's Great Famine was read for this story. Dikötter's numbers reach it through his own 2010 newspaper article and Cormac Ó Gráda's review, which is a sharp critic of him; Yang Jisheng's through NPR and Ian Johnson's review. The lower estimates by demographers were not read in the original either, only as Ó Gráda lists them. Death tolls are given as ranges with the author and what the number counts. Words such as “genocide” and “camps” appear only where a named person or body used them. A motion in one chamber of a parliament, or one government's determination, is not a court ruling, and the story does not treat it as one.
