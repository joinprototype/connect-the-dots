title: The Frame
subtitle: How one company came to make and sell so many of the best-known glasses, and to run a U.S. vision plan, what glasses cost, and whether sunglasses do what they say
summary: In 1929 a pair of sunglasses sold for ten cents on a boardwalk. Since then an Italian company, now EssilorLuxottica, has become, the European Commission said in 2018, the largest supplier of eyewear worldwide. This story follows that rise, what regulators in Brussels, Washington, Rome and Paris found, what the company and its critics say, and what eye-health bodies say sunglasses can do.
---

In 1929 a pair of sunglasses was a ten-cent buy on a boardwalk, and in the 1930s a glare-cutter for pilots. Today one group, EssilorLuxottica, owns Ray-Ban and Oakley, makes frames for luxury houses under licence and runs stores. In 2012 CBS reported that Luxottica also owned a U.S. vision plan, EyeMed.

A frame maker could now reach a customer through a brand, a shop and a vision plan. That reach brought questions about prices and competition: television reporters challenged executives, opticians described pressure to hold prices, and regulators sometimes cleared purchases and sometimes imposed fines. Behind every purchase lay another question for the wearer: what protection did the lenses actually provide?
