summary: "A popular version says the U.S. \"sponsored\" the Nazis, Japan and the Soviets. This story tests it deal by deal, keeping the U.S. government apart from companies and the years before December 1941 apart from the war years, with each side’s own words."
---

American companies sold factory designs to the Soviet Union from 1929, shared patents with German firms and owned factories that supplied Germany’s war. Washington allowed oil exports to Japan until 1941, then froze its assets; after Germany invaded the Soviet Union, Roosevelt sent aid under Lend-Lease.

Who sold, who decided, and when matters. A company contract, a government embargo and an alliance were different acts, with different consequences for soldiers, civilians and people forced to work in the factories. The claim that the U.S. “sponsored” all three regimes has to answer for those differences.
