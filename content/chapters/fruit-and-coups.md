summary: "Governments fell in Iran in 1953, Guatemala in 1954 and Chile in 1973, in countries where a British oil company, an American fruit company and an American telephone company had interests at stake. Here is what the CIA, the State Department and the Senate show about each, and where the accounts part."
---
Iran in 1953, Guatemala in 1954, Chile in 1973: in each, by its own records, the U.S. government ran covert action against the government that fell, and in each a foreign company's interests were in dispute. A popular version says the companies were behind it; even the CIA's own historian of Guatemala notes that "some accuse" the government of acting at a company's behest.

Oil remained nationalized in Iran, but foreign companies returned to operate it. In Guatemala, United Fruit backed an armed challenger while Washington pursued its own covert operation. In Chile, ITT offered money to stop Allende, and the CIA advised it on funding his opponent even while refusing to take the money itself. The reasons diverged too: Mossadegh spoke of work and food, Árbenz of economic independence, and U.S. officials of Communism, compensation and the danger that Allende's example might spread. Their decisions reached far beyond company balance sheets.
