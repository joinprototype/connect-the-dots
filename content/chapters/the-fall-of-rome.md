---

The western Roman empire ended in the 400s. The eastern half went on until 1453. Why the west ended is one of the oldest arguments in history, and whether “fell” is even the right word is part of it: some historians describe a catastrophe, others a long change.

This story follows Rome from the Senate's offer of citizenship to its Italian allies, through the third-century crisis and the sacks of the city, to the explanations historians have given, each in its holder's name: the army and its cost, pressure from outside, the loss of living standards, the idea of transformation, and climate and disease. It does not choose between them. The ancient texts reach it in English translations. The modern historians reach it through their own essays and the reviews of their books, not the books themselves. A tale is told as a tale, and a motive is given as a reading. Dates are in the modern calendar.
