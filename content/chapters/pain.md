summary: Pills given for pain, two Purdue companies that pleaded guilty thirteen years apart, a family that denies liability, a consulting firm, and a Supreme Court ruling. Here is the road between, from plea papers, hearing transcripts and the opinion itself, with the families’ own words wherever the record has them.
---

Pain is why the pills were prescribed, and the word the families use. This story follows one drug, OxyContin, and the company that sold it.

It keeps apart what a court or an official body found, what a person said under oath, and what is only alleged. Purdue Pharma, the Purdue Frederick Company, individual Sackler family members, McKinsey, drug distributors and doctors are separate actors. Where a settlement ends without a finding, the story says so. None of the records read here shows a criminal charge against a Sackler family member. The FDA’s part in the 1995 approval and label is told in [User Fees](/c/user-fees/); this story judges neither. Counts differ by source, and the story gives each.
