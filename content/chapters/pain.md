summary: Pills given for pain, two Purdue companies that pleaded guilty thirteen years apart, a family that denies liability, a consulting firm, and a Supreme Court ruling. Here is the road between, from plea papers, hearing transcripts and the opinion itself, with the families’ own words wherever the record has them.
---

Pain is why the pills were prescribed, and the word the families use. This story follows one drug, OxyContin, and the company that sold it.

Two Purdue companies pleaded guilty thirteen years apart. Neither company plea charged a Sackler family member. The family denies liability, and its settlements resolved civil allegations without a determination of liability. Purdue Pharma, Purdue Frederick, individual family members, McKinsey, distributors and doctors made separate decisions. The FDA’s part in the 1995 approval and label is told in [User Fees](/c/user-fees/).
