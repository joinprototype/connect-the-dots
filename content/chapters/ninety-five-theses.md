title: Ninety-Five Theses
subtitle: How a Wittenberg teacher's objections to the sale of indulgences travelled by printing press to Worms, to a Bible in German, and to a Europe of divided churches
summary: In 1517 a university teacher in Wittenberg invited a debate on indulgences. Printers carried his words across Europe, a pope and an emperor answered, and by 1555 the churches of the Empire were divided. Here is the road, from letters, bulls and decrees, with each side in its own words.
---

In 1517 a theology lecturer at the University of Wittenberg wrote ninety-five propositions about the sale of indulgences. Within five years he had been condemned by a pope, heard by an emperor, and had put the New Testament into German.

[From Clay to Print](/c/from-clay-to-print/) ended with a press at Mainz and, at its last stop, a Bible in German printed at Wittenberg. This story goes back to find how that Bible came to be.

Each side is given in its own words, and the story does not say which was right. Where the sources disagree, as they do on whether the theses were nailed to a door, each account is given and credited. Luther’s later writings on the Jews are part of the record and are reported plainly, with the later words of the Lutheran church that bears his name.
