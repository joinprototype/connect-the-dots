title: The Great Dying
subtitle: Did the Americas suffer the worst catastrophe in history, and who answers for it? The estimates, the letters, the massacres and the court records
summary: By one count the Americas held 60 million people in 1492 and about nine in ten were gone by 1600. Some historians call it the worst catastrophe in history, and say it was done to them. Here are the estimates, where the diseases came from, what the documents show about war, removal and deliberate spread, the argument over the word genocide, and the records of today.
---

By one count the Americas held 60 million people in 1492, and about nine in ten were gone by 1600. Some historians call it the worst catastrophe in history, and say it was done to the people who lived there. Every number here is an estimate, and scholars disagree about nearly all of them.

The story is built from historians' own work, laws and treaties, committee reports, court opinions and the words of those named, each given its say. It states the claim first and tests it at the end. Where sources differ it gives the range and says who counted. It does not rank this history against any other, and it does not pick between the scholars who call it genocide and those who do not.
