summary: In 1621 the English at Plymouth feasted for three days with Ousamequin and about ninety men. Here is that meal in the one eyewitness account, two popular versions of it tested, and the record from Mistick Fort to the 2024 boarding school apology, from official reports and Native nations' own words.
---

In 1621 the English at Plymouth feasted for three days with Ousamequin and about ninety men. Only one eyewitness wrote it down.

Native nations' own words are used where we have them: the Mashpee Wampanoag Tribe and the Wampanoag Tribe of Gay Head open the story, and tribal governments, the Pequot museum's report and Chief John Ross speak in their scenes. Several other sources are English or American and one-sided, and the story says so. Where counts differ the range is shown with who counted. A colony, a settler crowd and the U.S. government are kept apart. The word massacre appears in the narration only where Congress, an official inquiry or the National Park Service uses it, or inside a quotation, and each time the story says who uses it.
