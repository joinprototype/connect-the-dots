title: The Meeting of 20 February
subtitle: Who paid for Hitler's rise to power, and what the record shows
summary: On 20 February 1933, Hitler and Göring asked German industrialists to finance an election they said would be the last for years. The men paid; after the war, their words and payments helped bring their choices before the courts.
---

On 20 February 1933, three weeks after Adolf Hitler became Chancellor, he and Hermann Göring met about two dozen industrialists at Göring’s house in Berlin. After they left, Hjalmar Schacht asked for 3 million Reichsmarks for the election of 5 March. A bank ledger records roughly 2 million, including 400,000 from IG Farben; Karl Heinz Roth puts the company’s full contribution at 500,000, divided between the Nazi leadership and Franz von Papen.

The industrialists paid after Hitler had taken office. How much business money helped him get there, and how willingly the men paid that evening, remain disputed. The telegram, speeches, bank entries and later testimony show people making choices: what Hitler offered, what the guests paid for, and how they explained themselves when the regime’s crimes came before the courts.
