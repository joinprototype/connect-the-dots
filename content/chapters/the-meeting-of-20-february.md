subtitle: Who paid for Hitler's rise to power, and what the record shows
summary: On 20 February 1933, three weeks after Hitler became Chancellor, he and Göring asked German industrialists for money before the March election. Here is that evening from the Nuremberg documents, what historians who disagree make of it, the money that followed, and what the regime did.
---

On 20 February 1933, three weeks after Adolf Hitler became Chancellor, he and Hermann Göring met about two dozen industrialists at Göring's house in Berlin. When the two had left, Hjalmar Schacht asked the guests for money for the election of 5 March.

That evening is on the record, in documents and sworn statements from the Nuremberg trials. How much it mattered, and who paid for the Nazis' rise before it, is argued. Here Henry Ashby Turner, who holds that big business did less before 1933 than often said, Adam Tooze, Peter Hayes (through a review of his latest book) and the Communist International each speak, and the story does not pick one. It does not cover Reinhard Neebe's study of the Reich Association of German Industry. Where a source is a memory sworn years later, or an allegation, the story says so; the Nuremberg Judgment's words on what the regime did are given plainly.
