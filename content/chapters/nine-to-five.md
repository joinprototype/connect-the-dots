title: Nine to Five
subtitle: How an eight-hour demand became a 40-hour overtime standard, and who was left outside it
summary: Workers struck for eight hours in 1886. Railroad unions won an eight-hour basis for a day’s pay in 1916, Ford put five eight-hour days together in 1926, and Congress brought the overtime threshold down to 40 hours in 1940 for workers the law covered.
---

Chicago strikers asked for eight hours of work, many at ten hours’ pay. Railroad unions won an eight-hour basis for a day’s wages, and Ford Motor Company put five eight-hour days on its own schedule. The federal rule Congress passed in 1938 worked through pay. By October 1940 it generally required time and a half after 40 hours for covered workers, leaving the days and clock hours up to workplaces.
