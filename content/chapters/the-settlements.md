summary: "A baby powder, a contact lens solution, an acne drug, a drug-price hearing, a mercury factory and hair relaxers, with a drug-trial case for contrast. Here is what juries, appeals courts, regulators and the companies themselves said about who was hurt, who sued, what was paid and what was admitted."
---

Gail Ingham went to court over ovarian cancer. Contact lens wearers sued after infections cost some of them an eye. Workers in Kodaikanal asked for healthcare after years around mercury. Their cases brought company documents, scientific studies and personal losses before judges.

A jury could award damages, an appeals court could overturn them, and a company could pay a settlement while denying fault. For the people seeking help, each decision determined whether money would come, whether testimony would be heard, and how much longer they would wait.
