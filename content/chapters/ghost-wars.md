subtitle: Who paid whom in Afghanistan, from 1979 to 2021
summary: Carter authorized aid, Pakistan chose recipients, and Saudi Arabia paid alongside Washington. Later, bin Laden bought refuge from the Taliban, and U.S. war and aid money reached its enemies.
---
Washington and Riyadh supplied billions of dollars to Afghan rebels fighting the Soviet occupation. Pakistan distributed the aid and favored particular factions. Later, Human Rights Watch reports, Saudi Arabia backed the Taliban; the 9/11 Commission estimates bin Laden paid its rulers $10–20 million a year for refuge. Security contractors used U.S. funds to pay insurgents to leave convoys alone, and after the Taliban returned to power, 38 U.S.-funded aid groups reported at least $10.9 million in taxes, fees and utilities.

The people making those decisions gave different reasons: resisting Moscow, securing a border, opening trade routes, sheltering fighters, getting supplies through, keeping aid flowing. Their words and the money’s destinations show how each deal kept shaping Afghanistan long after it was made.
