---

After 11 September 2001 the United States fought wars in Afghanistan and Iraq. A common argument says enemies were made on purpose, so that arms makers and contractors could take trillions. This story has no single holder of that argument to quote.

It checks the money and the public claims against records: the 9/11 Commission, the Iraq Survey Group, the Senate Intelligence Committee, the Wartime Contracting Commission, the government's watchdog for Afghanistan, and the Costs of War Project's counts at Brown University. Where sources differ, it says who counted. A company, a government and a person are kept apart. It shows how much was spent, who was paid, how much was lost, and what was said at the time. It reads no internal record of how the decisions were made, so it cannot say why the wars were fought. Motive is only ever given as someone's own account.
