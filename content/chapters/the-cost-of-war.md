The people who chose, fought and supplied these wars explain their reasons; official inquiries trace the costs and failures.
---

After 11 September 2001, the United States fought wars in Afghanistan and Iraq. By September 2021, Neta Crawford put the spending and future obligations of the post-9/11 wars at about $8 trillion, including veterans’ care and interest. William Hartung counted over $2.1 trillion in Pentagon contracts to five weapons makers in fiscal 2001–2020; the Wartime Contracting Commission estimated $31–$60 billion lost to waste and fraud in Iraq and Afghanistan. Those totals count different things.

The decisions have people behind them: Bin Ladin naming his enemies, Bush asking for war, an executive demanding funds, an officer signing a contract, an Afghan official refusing an unsafe road. Their words explain the reasons they gave. The payments, the losses and the inquiries show what followed.
