subtitle: How a war in October 1973 became an embargo, a price shock, and lines at American gas stations
summary: In October 1973 the Arab members of OPEC cut output and stopped oil to the United States. Prices quadrupled and Americans queued for gasoline. Here is the road from OPEC's founding to the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, from declassified State Department records, statutes, and the words of those who were there.
---

In October 1973 a war in the Middle East ended with an embargo on oil to the United States. By January 1974 the price of a barrel had nearly quadrupled, and in the winter that followed Americans queued at gas stations.

Nearly every line here comes from the State Department's published record of the time: cables, memos, and transcripts of telephone calls, with the Federal Reserve's history, the statutes, and the organizations' own pages beside them. Where governments gave different accounts of the war and its causes, the story gives each its own words and does not pick one.
