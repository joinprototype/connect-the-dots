title: "Midnight, 1947"
subtitle: "A union proposed, a date brought forward, a border published after independence"
summary: "British India was divided into India and Pakistan in August 1947; millions fled, while leaders disputed borders, protection and the right to return."
---

In August 1947 British India became two countries, India and Pakistan, and the provinces of Punjab and Bengal were cut in two. The new border was published two days after independence.

Millions crossed it. A 2008 census study by the economists Prashant Bharadwaj, Asim Ijaz Khwaja and Atif Mian estimates that 17.9 million people left their homes and that 14.5 million migrants, with the children born to them since, were living on the other side by 1951; Jammu and Kashmir are not counted. The 3.4 million gap includes the dead and those who went elsewhere, the authors write, so it is not a count of the dead.

The number of dead is disputed. Estimates run from 200,000, the British figure at the time, to two million, a later Indian estimate, Urvashi Butalia wrote in 1998; she called about a million widely accepted, a figure the historian Gyanendra Pandey questions.
