title: Jekyll Island
subtitle: How the Federal Reserve was made, from a secret duck hunt to a law, and what the tales add
summary: In 1910 six men kept it secret that they were going to an island off Georgia to draft a central bank. Three years later Congress passed a law that kept much of the plan’s machinery and changed who controls it, by the Fed’s own account. Here is what the record shows about who made the Federal Reserve, who runs, owns and audits it, and a Titanic tale that no record supports.
---

In November 1910 six men kept it secret that they were going to an island off the coast of Georgia to write a plan for a central bank. In December 1913 President Wilson signed a law that created one.

Nearly every line comes from the Federal Reserve’s own history essays, the text of the Act, its financial statements and the words of those in the room, set beside the scholars, journalists and fact-checkers who tested the tales. The Fed’s account of itself is its account; where critics and the Fed disagree, each gets its say. A banker’s seat at a meeting is a connection, not evidence of wrongdoing, and the tales that need more than that are named as tales.
