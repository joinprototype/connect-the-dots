title: The Deported
subtitle: Whole peoples moved by order of the Soviet state, 1937 to 1944, and what the records say about who gave the orders, who died, who came home
summary: Between 1937 and 1944 the Soviet state moved whole peoples, from Koreans in the Far East to the Chechens, Ingush and Crimean Tatars, to Kazakhstan, Central Asia and Siberia. Here are the decrees, the NKVD's own counts and the scholars who dispute them, Khrushchev's partial admission in 1956, and who was and was not allowed home.
---

Between 1937 and 1944 the Soviet government moved whole peoples from their homes on the strength of decrees, many of them secret. This chapter follows those orders, the NKVD's own reports, and what historians and parliaments have since made of them.

It covers the Koreans (1937), the Volga Germans (1941), the Karachays, Kalmyks, Chechens, Ingush and Balkars (1943-44), the Crimean Tatars and the Meskhetian Turks (1944). It does not tell the removal of the Poles in 1940, or of the Finns, Greeks, Bulgarians, Armenians and Kurds: the sources here give them no account of their own. The 1989 declaration names more peoples than this chapter can follow.

Deaths are given with who counted them and what the count covers, and where sources disagree both figures stand. Some peoples have only one figure, and where a people has none in these sources the chapter says so. A charge of treason is told as a charge, with who made it and what the state later did with it. What a document says, what a scholar concluded and what is only alleged are kept apart.
