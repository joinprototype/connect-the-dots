title: Quid Pro Quo
subtitle: Who funds American politicians, who lobbies them, and where the law draws the line
summary: Some say paid lobbying is plain corruption; others say it is citizens petitioning their government. The law has its own word, and only a court can apply it. Here is how that word was drawn, narrowed and tested, what lobbyists and critics say, and what the studies show.
---

Some people say that paid lobbying is just a plain form of corruption. Others say it is how citizens are heard. This story does not take a side. It follows the line the law drew between influence and bribery, case by case, and then looks at the evidence on whether money changes what government does.

Only a court's conviction makes something bribery, and the courts have narrowed what counts. Every number comes from a named source, and each person is described by what the record shows. Where scholars disagree, each has their say. The campaign money behind the Watergate break-in, which led to the 1974 law, is told in [The Plumbers](/c/the-plumbers/).
