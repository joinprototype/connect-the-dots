title: Quid Pro Quo
subtitle: Who funds American politicians, who lobbies them, and where the law draws the line
summary: Some say paid lobbying is plain corruption; others say it is citizens petitioning their government. The law has its own word, and only a court can apply it. Here is how that word was drawn, narrowed and tested, what lobbyists and critics say, and what the studies show.
---

House and Senate candidates reported raising $3.8 billion in the 2024 election cycle, according to the Federal Election Commission. OpenSecrets counted another kind of spending: $4.4 billion on federal lobbying that year. Donors financed campaigns; paid advocates sought hearings. Gifts to candidates, independent election spending and lobbying each followed different rules.

Lobbyists defended their work as citizens petitioning government. Critics saw dependence on funders even where no law was broken. Donors and officials tested the boundary, and judges narrowed what counted as corruption. The campaign money behind the Watergate break-in, which led to the 1974 law, is told in [The Plumbers](/c/the-plumbers/).
