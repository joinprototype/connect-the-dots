subtitle: What became of Standard Oil's pieces after 1911, who holds them now, and whether anything still links them
summary: In 1911 the Supreme Court broke Standard Oil into what are usually counted as 34 companies, and the stock went back to its owners. Here is what became of the pieces, from the decree to the FTC's board-seat orders of 2024 and 2025, and what the record does and does not show about whether they still act as one.
---

In 1911 the Supreme Court ordered Standard Oil broken up. The stock of the pieces went back to its owners, in proportion, under an order not to recombine. Today two of the heirs, Exxon and Chevron, are among the largest oil companies on earth.

This story follows the pieces from the decree to the board seats the FTC fought over in 2024 and let go in 2025, and asks one plain question: is it still one thing? No court has found that it is. So the story sets out what each side can show, from court opinions, the FTC's own papers, the companies' proxy statements and the economists' studies. A shared origin or a name on the same shareholder list is not evidence of control, or of wrongdoing, and no source here shows either.
