A name in Jeffrey Epstein’s book proves nothing. So which presidents, princes, ministers and professors actually dealt with him, what does the record show for each, what does each say, and what has it cost them? Then the fight over his files: who pushed, who resisted, what was released and what was held back.

This story was read in the first days of November 2026, and the record is still moving, so cards give the date of the latest report read where it matters, and the last card lists later steps that are known to exist and were not read. It rests on court filings, government documents and the people’s own words, and on established newspapers where those are not available. A mention is not a dealing, a dealing is not knowledge of a crime, a connection is not evidence of wrongdoing, and an arrest is not a charge or a finding. Where an allegation is told, it is told as one, with who made it and the reply. The Justice Department’s files also hold unverified tips; a tip is not evidence, and none is repeated here. Survivors who spoke under their own names speak first.
