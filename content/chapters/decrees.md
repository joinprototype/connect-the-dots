title: Decrees
subtitle: From Spain’s order to leave in 1492 to Portugal’s forced baptisms in 1497
summary: Ferdinand and Isabella ordered Jews out by July 1492. Some converted; others sold their homes and fled. A Spanish marriage condition carried the demand into Portugal, where Manuel promised passage, then forced baptism. The decrees, an exile chronicle and historians’ competing explanations trace how it happened.
---

On 31 March 1492, Ferdinand and Isabella signed the order. By July’s end, every Jew had to leave their kingdoms or face death and confiscation. Abraham Seneor and Isaac Abravanel tried to secure permission to stay; one accepted baptism, the other left.

The road follows that choice through the sales, the ships and Portugal’s border, then into a royal marriage agreement and the gathering at Lisbon. Each ruler gave terms; families paid for decisions they could not control.
