On the morning of 11 September 2001 al Qaeda hijackers flew airliners into the World Trade Center and the Pentagon, and a fourth crashed in Pennsylvania; the 9/11 Commission counted 2,973 dead. The official record of who did it, and of what went wrong before, is the Commission's report.

Flight attendants called from hijacked planes. Passengers voted to fight back. Peter Hayden later told the Commission they expected to lose firefighters but had to try to rescue civilians. Years later, families were still asking who had helped the attackers, while Saudi Arabia denied supporting them and sought to have their lawsuit dismissed.
