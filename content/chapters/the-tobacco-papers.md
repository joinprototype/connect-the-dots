In December 1953 Paul Hahn called the cigarette chiefs to New York to answer the cancer studies. They hired a public relations firm and told smokers they believed their products were not injurious to health. In 2006 Judge Gladys Kessler found a decades-long scheme to defraud smokers and potential smokers.

Rose Cipollone sued while she had lung cancer; her son carried on after she and her husband died. States later recovered money for smoking-related health costs through a settlement that admitted no wrongdoing. Company reports and memoranda brought internal assessments into court beside public denials, and gave researchers in other industries questions to pursue.
