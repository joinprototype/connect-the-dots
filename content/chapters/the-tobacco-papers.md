In December 1953 the heads of the American cigarette makers met in New York and hired a public relations firm. Fifty-three years later a federal judge ruled on what followed.

Nearly every line here comes from a court opinion, the 1998 settlement text, the 1964 Surgeon General’s report and internal company papers as the court quotes them. Where a company, a trade group, a public relations firm and an agency each acted, the story says which. A settlement is not a verdict, and the story says what the agreement says. The companies’ own words get their say. Where researchers study sugar and oil, the story says what they found and what they did not claim.
