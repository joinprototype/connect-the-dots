title: The Berlin Conference
subtitle: How fourteen powers made rules for claiming African territory, and what it took for Germany to hold its claim in the southwest
summary: From the General Act of 1885 to the proposed Germany–Namibia declaration of 2021, follow what it took for Germany to hold its claim in Southwest Africa and what descendants of the people it targeted asked of Germany.
---

On 15 November 1884, Otto von Bismarck opened a conference at 77 Wilhelmstraße in Berlin. A dispute over the mouth of the Congo River had brought the powers together. Bismarck had already declared a protectorate in Southwest Africa that April, according to historian Adam Blackler. The conference set rules for new coastal claims, then Britain and Germany set the limits of Germany’s southwestern sphere in a separate treaty in 1890. German forces held the claim through war and genocide.
