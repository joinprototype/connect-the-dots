subtitle: The deportations and killings of Ottoman Armenians, what survivors, diplomats and courts recorded, who calls it genocide, and what Turkey says in its own words
summary: In 1915 the Ottoman government deported most of its Armenian subjects, and its agents killed very many. Here are the survivors who testified, the ambassador's cables, the Ottoman court-martial of 1919, the Berlin trial of 1921, Lemkin's use of the case, the numbers with who counted them, who calls it genocide, and Turkey's position stated and answered.
---

From 1915 the Ottoman government deported most of its Armenian subjects, and its officials, soldiers and irregulars killed very many of them, the U.S. Holocaust Memorial Museum says. The Museum counts at least 664,000 dead and possibly 1.2 million. Turkey's foreign ministry says slightly less than 600,000 Anatolian Armenians died over 1912 to 1922, and rejects the word genocide.

Bishop Krikoris Balakian survived arrest and deportation, then travelled to Berlin to testify. Christine Terzibashian told the same court how her family tried to keep one another alive. Their testimony, the ambassador’s cables and the Ottoman court-martial’s verdict sit alongside the reasons Talaat gave for the policy and Turkey’s continuing rejection of the word genocide.
