---

How did home loans become a public rescue, and who answered for it? American home prices peaked in 2006. Two years later Lehman Brothers was bankrupt and Congress was voting on $700 billion.

Lenders sold mortgages, bankers turned them into bonds, and investors trusted the ratings. When borrowers could no longer pay, losses reached banks around the world. Officials chose which firms to rescue and which to let fail. By January 2011, the Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission counted about four million families who had lost homes to foreclosure, and nearly $11 trillion in household wealth lost. Treasury’s final TARP ledger would record $443.5 billion disbursed and $425.5 billion collected. Criminal cases brought acquittals and some prison sentences, while the government settled major civil cases; the commission’s ten members could not agree on which decisions had caused the disaster.
