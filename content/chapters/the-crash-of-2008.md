subtitle: How home loans became bonds, the bonds brought down banks, and a commission could not agree on why
summary: American home prices peaked in 2006. Two years later Lehman Brothers was bankrupt and Congress was voting on $700 billion. Here is the road between, from the official inquiry, the court's examiner, and the words of those in the room.
---

American home prices peaked in 2006. Two years later Lehman Brothers was bankrupt and Congress was voting on $700 billion.

Lenders sold mortgages, bankers turned them into bonds, and investors trusted the ratings. When borrowers could no longer pay, losses reached banks around the world. Officials chose which firms to rescue and which to let fail. By January 2011, the Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission counted about four million families who had lost homes to foreclosure, and nearly $11 trillion in household wealth lost. Its ten members could not agree on why it happened.
