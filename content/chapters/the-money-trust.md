---

In 1912 a House committee asked whether a few New York bankers held the country's money. This story follows them, and the steel and oil men on the same boards, to the places where the record shows things happened: a dam, a steel works, a company town, a prison mine, a mining camp.

Nearly every line comes from official records, sworn testimony, and the words the men wrote or said themselves, set beside each other. A bank board seat or a shared stock is a connection, not evidence of any act. Where a record names a person as having ordered or done something, the story says so; where it was a company's men, the guards, the Guard, or a state, it says that instead, and where no order is shown it says that too. Where accounts differ, each gets its say, and the story does not choose between them.
