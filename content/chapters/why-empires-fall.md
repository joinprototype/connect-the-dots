title: Why Empires Fall
subtitle: From Augustine to Gorbachev, the explanations people gave while their world came apart
summary: "After Rome's sack, Augustine answered people who blamed Christianity. Procopius described plague and accused an emperor; Qing and Ottoman rulers defended choices later judged against their losses. Gorbachev defended reform, Gaidar traced dependence on grain and credit, and Miller argued that political conflict defeated reform."
---

A city can be taken without an empire ending. A ruler can call his country rich while his government cannot pay for imported grain. When people explained a fall, what had failed: people's safety, a ruler's freedom to act, or the state's ability to keep governing?

Augustine answered an accusation after [Rome's sack](/c/the-fall-of-rome/); Procopius referred plague to God but accused an emperor of destroying his subjects. [Qing rulers](/c/the-great-divergence/) ceded territory and opened ports; [Ottoman officials](/c/the-ottomans/) surrendered revenue to creditors while their state survived. [Gorbachev](/c/rise-and-fall-of-the-soviet-union/) defended reform even as he resigned; Gaidar later traced the Soviet ending through grain and debt, while Miller argued that political conflict defeated reform.
