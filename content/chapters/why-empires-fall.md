title: Why Empires Fall
subtitle: The people who bargained, fought and reformed as their states came apart
summary: "Thirteen scenes follow the choices behind imperial endings: buying grain, paying soldiers, defending a capital, sharing a throne and surrendering power. Roman, Ottoman, Mongol and Soviet decisions meet the arguments over why their states fell, with Byzantine and Qing comparisons."
---

An army needs pay, a city needs grain, and a ruler needs people willing to obey. When those fail, someone still has to choose: give soldiers land, ask foreign governments for credit, surrender a monopoly on power, or fight for a capital that may be lost.

These thirteen scenes follow decisions behind the endings of [western Rome](/c/the-fall-of-rome/), [the Ottoman state](/c/the-ottomans/), [the Mongol realm](/c/the-mongols/) and [the Soviet Union](/c/rise-and-fall-of-the-soviet-union/). The [Qing defeat in the First Opium War](/c/the-great-divergence/) shows a loss of power within a dynasty that continued. Money, invasion, disease and reform enter through the people who faced them; the disagreements over what brought each state down remain in their holders’ names.
