summary: Historians disagree about why Rome’s west, the Ottoman Empire, the Mongol realm, the Yuan and the Soviet Union ended. Thirteen comparisons bring their explanations together, with the Byzantine and Qing evidence where it fits, and the argument over whether an empire fell or changed.
---

Why do empires end? Historians do not agree. This story brings together explanations already told in the record, in the names of the historians, witnesses and participants who give them. It does not settle their disagreements or propose a rule of history.

A division, a lost capital, a dynasty’s removal and a state’s dissolution are different endings in these accounts. The Qing material concerns the Great Divergence and the First Opium War; it does not supply an account of the dynasty’s final end. The comparisons keep the limits of the original stories: ancient and medieval witnesses in English translation, modern arguments sometimes reached through reviews, and no new research or sources. Five quoted passages inherited from the Soviet story could not be located in the saved research; four other inherited passages have OCR or punctuation differences. Those remain verification limits, not findings that the quotations were invented.
