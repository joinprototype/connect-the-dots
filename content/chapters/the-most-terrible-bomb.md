---

In 1939 a physicist warned a president about a new kind of bomb. Six years later it fell on two cities.

The two bombs killed between 110,000 and 210,000 people, across the most credible estimates. Stimson later said his purpose was victory at the least cost in American soldiers’ lives; Szilard warned that using the bomb could open an era of devastation. Families beneath it faced the consequences. Their reasons—and their disagreements—survived the surrender.
