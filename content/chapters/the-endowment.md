summary: "Endowments hold tens of billions of dollars, tax-exempt, built on gifts. Is that money sent back to donors? Here is what the law, the schools’ own reports, a court, a Senate inquiry and the press show, and what they do not."
---

Harvard offers donors contracts that pay income for life, outside its endowment. A different allegation—that endowment management fees flow back to colleges as donations—prompted an anonymous complaint about Dartmouth in 2012. Dartmouth denied wrongdoing, and New Hampshire officials found no basis to pursue the complaint further.

A donor’s contract promises income; a manager earns fees for investing; an admissions preference helps an applicant get a place. Behind each are people making choices: a couple planning a gift, a trustee selecting investments, an admissions dean weighing an applicant, and a senator asking why students still struggle to pay tuition.
