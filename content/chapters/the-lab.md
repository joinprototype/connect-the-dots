title: The Lab
subtitle: When a professor, a spouse or a sponsor holds a stake in what a university lab finds
summary: In 1980 Congress made it routine for universities to own and license what federal money paid for. Here are eight documented cases of personal and family money near public research, what each court, university and person said, and what studies can and cannot say about how common it is.
---

A professor can hold shares in the company that licenses discoveries from his lab. A spouse can run a firm receiving public research grants. Across these eight cases, neither a court nor a university found a lab selling research to a relative’s company. The cases do show financial ties, failed disclosures, a fatal trial, civil liability and criminal convictions.

Since 1980 U.S. universities have routinely patented and licensed federally funded inventions. Stanford’s DNA-cloning patent application came earlier, in 1974. Three cases bring the question about family companies closest: Genovo, the Harvard Russia project and an Oklahoma couple’s grant-funded firm. The studies measure broader financial ties and sponsorship, without counting sales to relatives’ companies. A connection is not evidence of wrongdoing.
