title: The Lab
subtitle: When a professor, a spouse or a sponsor holds a stake in what a university lab finds
summary: In 1980 Congress made it routine for universities to own and license what federal money paid for. Here are eight documented cases of personal and family money near public research, what each court, university and person said, and what studies can and cannot say about how common it is.
---

Since 1980 U.S. universities have routinely patented and licensed what federal money paid for. Money near research did not begin with that law: Stanford filed its DNA-cloning patent in 1974. The story does not blame the law for what follows.

We searched court records, congressional and university statements, the press and studies for professors, spouses and sponsors with a stake in what a lab found. We read eight cases and say what each court, university and person said, with every settlement marked "no admission". The question is the one many ask: do labs sell research to relatives' companies? Three of the cases touch it closely. None shows a court or a university finding that one did, and no study we read counts how often it happens. A connection is not evidence of wrongdoing.
