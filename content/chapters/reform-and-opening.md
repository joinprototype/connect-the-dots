summary: "From the Party's turn in December 1978 to the 2018 end of presidential term limits: the World Bank's and the Chinese government's figures, Xiaogang, Shenzhen, the one-child limit, Tiananmen, the layoffs, the WTO, the migrants, the stimulus, the debt, and the rival explanations, each in its holder's name."
---

By the World Bank's figures, output (GDP) per person in China rose from about $157 in 1978 to about $12,951 in 2023, in current U.S. dollars, and almost 800 million people left extreme poverty on its line. China's own count uses a different line. The same years brought mass layoffs, a national limit on births with forced abortions on the record, and in 1989 troops firing on protesters in Beijing.

Every number here has an owner. Where Chinese government figures and outside figures differ, both are given and the story says who counted. The Party, the state, firms and people are kept apart. Whose reading explains the rise is argued, and the story does not pick one. Living leaders appear only through documented acts and their own public words.
