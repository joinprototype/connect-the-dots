title: The Formula
subtitle: What the record shows about Nestlé, infant formula and babies in poor countries, from a Bern court to the WHO code
summary: In 1974 a British charity's report on baby milk became "Nestlé Kills Babies" in Switzerland. A court, a Senate hearing, a boycott and a World Health Assembly vote followed. Here is what the record shows, from the Bern excerpts, the hearing transcript and Nestlé's own replies.
---

A Bern court found in 1976 that powdered milk prepared with dirty water, dirty bottles or too little powder can kill an infant, that Nestlé's advertising in poor countries had gone further than at home, and that Nestlé would have to change it to be spared the charge of immoral conduct. It also found that the quality of Nestlé's milk was not contested, and that in criminal law Nestlé could not be held responsible for the deaths, because third parties' actions broke the chain. WHO's 1981 code says formula is needed when mothers do not breastfeed, and asks that its marketing be restrained.

Studies since, in Brazil and five other countries, show far higher risk of death from infection for babies not breastfed. The estimates count different things: 10 million cases a year of illness that breastfeeding could avoid in developing countries (1978, partly a guess), a million infant deaths a year (UNICEF, 1981), and 823,000 under-5 deaths a year in a 2016 Lancet model. None counts one company's customers, and no source read here counts deaths by company.

The story follows mothers and babies first, then the court, the 1978 Senate hearing, the boycott, the 1981 code and the monitors, with Nestlé's answers in its own words. Abbott, Bristol-Myers and American Home Products are separate companies and are kept apart. Nestlé's own website blocked automated fetches, so its words come from its 1978 testimony, its 1984 release, its 2007 reply, an archived page and a 2024 trade report. The Bern text is excerpts, translated and published by the group whose members were convicted; the German original was not found.
