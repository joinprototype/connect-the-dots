subtitle: How the Union began, and why it ended, as economists, Gorbachev, Washington and Moscow each explain it
summary: The Soviet Union ended in December 1991, and historians still disagree about why. One economist says grain and oil, another says politics, Gorbachev named a reactor and a coup, Washington named Gorbachev's reforms, and the Kremlin names Lenin's right to leave.
---

The Soviet Union ended in December 1991. Its leaders and later writers offered different explanations for its collapse. Yegor Gaidar, later Russia's acting prime minister, points to grain, oil and an empty treasury. Chris Miller, author of a book on Gorbachev's economic reforms, points to politics. Gorbachev named Chernobyl and the August coup. The U.S. State Department's history names Gorbachev's loosening of control and the coup. Vladimir Putin names Lenin's right to secede, and the historian Serhii Plokhy names one vote in Ukraine.

The Party's retreat also mattered: in the Library of Congress study's explanation, ending its monopoly removed the force holding the Union together. Reagan's arms build-up and missile defence belong to a related argument about the end of the Cold War. Plokhy gives internal Soviet struggles far more weight than American policy in explaining why the Union itself fell.
