summary: "From 1932 to the 1970s U.S. government programs experimented on people who had not agreed to it. Here are four of them, in the words of the inquiries that later examined them, the people involved, and the presidents who apologised."
---

In 1947 a U.S. military tribunal at Nuremberg wrote down the first rule of medical experiments: the subject’s voluntary consent. In the same years, and for decades after, U.S. government agencies ran experiments on people who had not agreed to them.

Nearly every line here comes from the government’s own later inquiries, the apologies of presidents, and the testimony of those involved, with a few from the writers Carl Elliott and Susan Reverby, named where used. Where accounts differ, each is given. Some records were destroyed for good, and the story says which; some were not read, and it says that too. It does not say who was guilty of anything no court or official inquiry found.
