title: Without Consent
subtitle: "Tuskegee, Guatemala, MKULTRA and the radiation experiments: what U.S. government inquiries found, and who apologised"
summary: "From 1932 to the 1970s U.S. government programs experimented on people who had not agreed to it. Here are four of them, in the words of the inquiries that later examined them, the people involved, and the presidents who apologised."
---

In Guatemala, U.S.-backed researchers deliberately exposed 1,308 people to sexually transmitted diseases. In Alabama, the Public Health Service followed 600 Black men, 399 with syphilis and 201 without it, while withholding treatment. CIA drug tests and plutonium injections into eighteen hospital patients extended the experiments into safe houses and hospital wards.

Doctors, officials and presidents left words explaining their choices: a plea for secrecy, a claim that more could still be learned, an apology for what the government had done. The people used in these experiments had choices taken from them. Some lived to demand an answer.
