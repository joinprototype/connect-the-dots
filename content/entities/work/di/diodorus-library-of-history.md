Diodorus wrote roughly three centuries after Alexander. C. H. Oldfather dates his research to the middle of the first century BCE. Waldemar Heckel identifies Cleitarchus’s lost history as the chief source for Book 17, supplemented by others; Diodorus and Curtius therefore do not always offer independent testimony. See [Oldfather’s introduction](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diodorus_Siculus/Introduction*.html) and [Heckel, pp. 8–9](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/42471/excerpt/9780521842471_excerpt.pdf).

For Books 18–20 on the successors, Oldfather identifies Hieronymus of Cardia’s lost history as Diodorus’s principal source. Hieronymus served Eumenes and then Antigonus; this is a different transmission from Book 17 ([Oldfather, p. xxii](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diodorus_Siculus/Introduction*.html)).
