In 2012 a group calling itself “The Friends of Eleazar Wheelock” wrote to state officials that Dartmouth trustees who run investment firms were paid high fees from the endowment and recycled part of them to the college as donations. The college called the letter inaccurate, said the investments were legal and proper, and said it began investing with those firms before the people joined the board. On 12 October 2012 the [[new-hampshire-attorney-general]]’s Charitable Trusts director wrote that the office found no basis to conclude the trustees had violated state law, and that the claims did not merit further investigation. That is a decision not to pursue the matter, as Dartmouth reports it; it is not a finding on whether the investments were good value.
