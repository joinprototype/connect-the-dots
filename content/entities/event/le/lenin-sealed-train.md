In April 1917 [[vladimir-lenin]] and some thirty other exiles travelled from [[zurich]] across Germany to Sweden and on to [[petrograd]]. Their carriage was treated as extraterritorial ("sealed").

Both sides recorded the deal. Lenin's group said the Swiss socialist [[fritz-platten]] negotiated it with the German ambassador; the [[german-imperial-government]]'s files show the foreign ministry and the General Staff approving it, with the army's [[german-army-high-command|High Command]] raising no objection. Accounts differ on what Berlin granted: Platten asked for a locked, extraterritorial carriage with himself as the only contact, and Berlin accepted his conditions "except Clause 2". A German report later that month added: "He is working exactly as we would wish."

Historians disagree about German motives and money; the chapter on this event gives each account with its source.
