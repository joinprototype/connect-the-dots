Peter’s order of March 1704 specified 40,000 workers. Paul Keenan describes armed escorts and a December 1712 Senate report on desertion. The order counts people demanded. A return discussed by E. A. Andreeva counts 27 deaths among 3,262 people on the road that became Nevsky Prospect in 1716. In a letter that year, Governor Alexander Menshikov reported to cabinet secretary Aleksei Makarov more than 1,000 deaths at Peterhof and Strelna, with no figure for the size of the workforce. Andreeva found 61 deaths among a separate group of 2,210 skilled workers sent to settle in the city in 1711–12. In the dispatch records Andreeva examined, officials replaced people who fled or fell ill, so those lists did not yield a consolidated death return.

Andreeva traces the claim that the city was built on bones to reports such as Danish envoy Just Juel’s. In 1710 he repeated hearsay of more than 100,000 deaths in the first year of construction, before he arrived. The later estimates cover different periods and use different methods. Andreeva describes O. G. Ageeva’s estimate of about 2,000 construction deaths in 1703–15, projected from one 1716 road return (27 out of 3,262 is about 0.83 percent, compared with the 0.74 percent printed in Andreeva’s account of Ageeva’s calculation). Andreeva estimated 13,500–18,000 deaths for 1703–12 by applying a 6–8 percent rate from fragmentary returns to about 227,000 workers in several categories. Robert K. Massie reports an estimate of 100,000 in Peter’s time and later figures of 25,000–30,000. [Andreeva’s study](https://ru.scribd.com/document/393836294/%25D0%2592%25D0%25BE%25D0%25BF%25D1%2580%25D0%25BE%25D1%2581%25D1%258B-%25D0%25A4%25D0%25B8%25D0%25BB%25D0%25BE%25D1%2581%25D0%25BE%25D1%2584%25D0%25B8%25D0%25B8-2013-06-%25D0%259D%25D0%25B5%25D1%2582-%25D0%25A1%25D1%2582%25D1%2580-40-45), [Massie’s account](https://erenow.org/modern/peterthegreat/27.php).

In 2020, V. A. Lapshin and N. F. Solovyova reported 255 skeletons excavated at an early-eighteenth-century burial site near the fortress. Doris Gutsmiedl-Schümann and her colleagues identified the people buried there as part of the forced construction workforce. That count covers a single burial site. [Excavation report](https://journal.fi/iskos/article/download/99582/57234), [interdisciplinary study](https://edoc.unibas.ch/entities/publication/08133476-3541-48d0-93c4-e41828dbfb11).
