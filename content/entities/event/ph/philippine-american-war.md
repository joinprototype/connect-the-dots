    note: "State also estimates over 20,000 Filipino combatants and as many as 200,000 civilians dead from violence, famine and disease, without giving a counting method. Blount extrapolates about 200,000 non-battlefield deaths. He compares a pre-occupation atlas estimate of Batangas’s population (312,192) with the 1903 census (257,715), and reproduces a provincial secretary’s December 1901 report of disease reducing over 300,000 people to a little over 200,000. Blount cautions that the latter is a neighborhood impression, not official statistics. The secretary’s December 1901 disease estimate predates Bell’s January 1902 campaign. These local population estimates are not registered deaths and are not added to national estimates. In his 1981 letter, John Gates uses Spanish population estimates and the 1903 census, adding all 137,505 cholera deaths reported for 1902, to propose a maximum of 321,000 war-related deaths for 1898–1903. This is his calculation’s ceiling. Stephen Shalom’s 1982 letter instead cites the census’s 1902 death rate, one in sixteen and twice normal, to suggest perhaps 10 percent of the population died during the war; that extrapolation concerns deaths during the war, not an individually registered or exclusively war-caused count. Daniel Immerwahr reports Ken De Bevoise’s estimate of about 775,000 deaths attributable to war in 1899–1903, many from disease. Princeton describes De Bevoise’s method as interpreting archival evidence through the health sciences; Immerwahr does not reproduce his demographic calculation. These estimates differ in periods, categories and assumptions; State’s civilian estimate is not an upper bound for them."

    note: "Blount describes Smith’s Samar campaign after Balangiga as devastation. Major Littleton Waller testified that Smith ordered killing and burning and designated ten as the age limit. Smith pleaded not guilty. The court found him guilty of conduct prejudicial to good order and military discipline, narrowing the killing instruction to persons capable of bearing arms and in actual hostilities, retaining the age limit. It sentenced him to admonition and stated that subordinates had neither understood nor executed the orders in their fullest implied sense. Roosevelt approved the findings and sentence on 14 July and ordered Smith’s retirement; the Army published the orders on 16 July. These are the court’s findings and mitigation, distinct from Waller’s testimony and Blount’s account."
