The figures describe different things. The Supreme Court’s opinion opens with New Jersey and 33 other corporate appellants. It describes the decree as binding New Jersey and 37 subsidiary corporations, and separately records dismissal of 33 other corporate defendants because their participation had not been proved. The Briscoe Center’s finding aid counts 34 distinct companies after dissolution. ([Opinion, pp. 30–31 and 45–46](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-221/pdf/USREPORTS-221-1.pdf); [Briscoe Center](https://collections.briscoecenter.org/repositories/2/resources/390).)

The decree barred the parent from voting the subsidiaries’ stock or controlling them. The shares could be distributed to the parent’s shareholders in proportion to their holdings, while the owners and companies were barred from recreating the illegal combination. Taft defended that remedy in his [5 December 1911 message](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/third-annual-message-17).

These are later paths of companies named in the decree, rather than names of newly created businesses:

| Company in the decree | Later path |
|---|---|
| [[standard-oil-new-jersey|Standard Oil of New Jersey]] | Renamed Exxon in 1972; merged with Mobil in 1999. |
| [[standard-oil-of-new-york|Standard Oil of New York]] and Vacuum Oil | Merged in 1931 as Socony-Vacuum; became [[mobil|Mobil]] in 1966, then joined Exxon in 1999. |
| [[standard-oil-of-california|Standard Oil of California]] | Acquired [[standard-oil-of-kentucky|Standard Oil of Kentucky]] in 1961; renamed [[chevron|Chevron]] in 1984. |
| [[standard-oil-of-indiana|Standard Oil of Indiana]] | Absorbed [[standard-oil-of-nebraska|Nebraska]] in 1939 and [[standard-oil-of-kansas|Kansas]] in 1948; renamed [[amoco|Amoco]] in 1985; merged with [[bp|BP]] in 1998. |
| [[standard-oil-of-ohio|Standard Oil of Ohio]] | BP completed its purchase in 1987. |
| [[ohio-oil-company|Ohio Oil Company]] | Renamed [[marathon-oil|Marathon Oil]] in 1962; acquired by [[conocophillips|ConocoPhillips]] in November 2024. |
| [[continental-oil-company|Continental Oil Company]] | ConocoPhillips identifies Continental Oil among its corporate roots. |
| Atlantic Refining | A predecessor of [[atlantic-richfield|ARCO]], named by Britannica among the trust’s descendants; ARCO’s merger with BP Amoco was subject to a 2000 FTC consent order. |

The renamings and early absorptions are recorded by [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Standard-Oil); [Chevron’s own timeline](https://www.chevron.com/about/history) records the 1961 Kentucky acquisition and 1984 renaming. The other paths are documented by [Ohio History Central](https://ohiohistorycentral.org/w/index.php?title=Ohio_Oil_Company&oldid=29173), [ConocoPhillips’ history](https://www.conocophillips.com/about-us/who-we-are/our-history/), the [Marathon acquisition announcement](https://www.worldoil.com/news/2024/11/22/conocophillips-completes-22-5-billion-acquisition-of-marathon-oil/), and the [FTC’s ARCO order](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2000/04/ftc-approves-final-order-bp-amoco-acquisition-arco).
