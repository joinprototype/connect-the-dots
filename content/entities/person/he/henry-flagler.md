In [testimony to an Ohio legislative committee in March 1879, printed by Tarbell in appendix 14](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/60692), Flagler offered to produce Standard’s railroad contracts confidentially, saying they contained business secrets: “None of these contracts provides for any discrimination whatever”. He credited Standard’s size, mechanical equipment and experience for its advantage, saying “it is idle to say that the small manufacturer can compete with us”; he denied that railroads had joined Standard in squeezing rivals out. Tarbell also prints O’Day’s 1878 letter demanding payments on every barrel the railroads carried, including competitors’ shipments, in her account of the [[railroad-drawbacks-of-1878]].
