In 1928 a Senate committee on public lands asked him about the Continental Trading Company and its profits. Time reported that in June 1928 a jury acquitted him of contempt for refusing to answer, and that in July a grand jury indicted him for perjury over his Teapot Dome testimony; the Annals of Iowa says he was tried twice, for contempt and then perjury, and acquitted each time. The Whiting-Robertsdale Historical Society says he was never charged or prosecuted; Time and the Annals contradict that.
