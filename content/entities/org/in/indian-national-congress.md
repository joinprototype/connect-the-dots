In his 3 June 1947 broadcast, Nehru defended Congress leaders’ acceptance of partition and recommended that its larger committees follow. He said the unity Congress sought was “a free and a willing association of a free people”, and hoped this choice might bring that unity sooner. [Nehru’s broadcast, pp. 98–99](https://nehruarchive.in/media/docs/selectedworks/volume-3-series-2/Volume-3-Second-Series.pdf).

In an April 30 press interview reproduced by the National Archives, Rajendra Prasad argued that if India were divided, the division should include Punjab and Bengal and be “as complete and thorough as possible” to leave no room for future conflict. Jinnah’s May 4 statement opposed dividing those provinces because it would disrupt their administrative, economic and political life. [Their statements](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/teaching-resources/indian-independence-part-two/).
