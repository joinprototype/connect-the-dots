# Changelog

What changed in each version of the site. [docs/deploy.md](docs/deploy.md#versions) says when the number goes up.

## 0.5.3 (2026-11-03)

- The old map's code is gone: about 5,800 lines and five packages (deck.gl, MapLibre), and about 3,200 files the site no longer needs. The home page's map picture is drawn from the islands, and pages may no longer fetch anything from another site.

## 0.5.2 (2026-11-03)

- The Map is one picture of everything read: every story an island, every entry a dot. Pick a dot and its links draw while the rest steps back, with each link in words beside it.
- "On Earth" moves whatever has a place onto the world: places, events, and companies where they are headquartered. People are never placed.
- A year typed in the box opens Ask's answer for that year.

## 0.5.1 (2026-11-03)

- One look on every page: black on white, whatever the system's dark mode says. Stories open on their title on white, covers and the footer are white, and nothing large is black any more.
- The header stays where it is on the Map, as on every other page.
- The Map draws only what has been read: 3,453 entries and their links, each with its proof. Open data, which nobody here has read, leaves it; it stays in search and on its own pages.

## 0.5.0 (2026-11-03)

- One view for each thing you do. Every story reads on Read, at its own address, over the world in dots; so do the stories drawn for a name or a question. Ask answers any two names. Explore and Connect are gone, and their old addresses take you to the same names on Stories, Ask or their pages.
- Ask shows what you can type: a name, two names, a year or a question, each with an example to tap.
- The maps beside a story come no nearer than a country, so the land still reads, and they name only the story's own places.
- The box no longer reads a word for a people as a name: "black people" is not Leon Black.

## 0.4.10 (2026-11-03)

- Read's pictures, and Ask's world, show their names again: since 0.4.9 each name sat under a black bar, because the look of the dots was written to a file no page loaded. The build now stops if that happens again.

## 0.4.9 (2026-11-03)

- Ask: one big box at /ask/. Type a name, two names, a year or a question, and the page becomes the answer, with its proof and its picture. It opens already asking how two things connect.
- Stories is one place you move on: every story an island, named at rest. Zoom in and its dots are named; click one and glide to it, with its words, links and proof beside it, and find the way from it to anything else. Only what a story is about lies on its island; whoever it shares or only mentions floats in the water nearby.
- Read: a story over the world drawn in dots, at /read/. Any name opens with its link and proof, and at the end the next story offers itself, with what it shares.

## 0.4.8 (2026-11-03)

- The Map, Stories and Explore are back in the header on every page, beside the box. Home shows the Map, and lists every way in.
- A story now ends on the stories that share the rarest names with it, each saying what it shares.

## 0.4.7 (2026-11-02)

- One box replaces the menu on every page. Type a name, two names, a year or a question. The footer keeps Stories, Map, Explore and Connect.
- The first page follows one name through the stories it runs through.
- Two names now get one answer, with their direct tie first, then the way between them, each step with its proof.
- Stories are drawn as islands, and any name in a story opens its page.
- Explore is now a walk: an entry beside its neighbours, each named, and one smooth move to the next. It opens far faster.
- A new story, Too Big to Fail, tests how much choice Americans really have, market by market.

## 0.4.6 (2026-11-02)

- Long stories start scrolling sooner, and a finger on a story's graph now scrolls the page. Text no longer jumps when its font arrives.
- Four stories on Jeffrey Epstein: Palm Beach, The Clients, The Address Book and Was He a Spy? Each tie shows exactly what the record holds, each person's reply, and that contact is not evidence of a crime.
- A link that only shows contact, or concerns a case, never carries a route, and unread open data stays off those pages.

## 0.4.5 (2026-11-02)

- Entries' details and their generated stories now share files, which takes the site from 18,034 files to 12,482 and leaves room for far more. Addresses stay the same.

## 0.4.4 (2026-11-02)

- On the Map, near now means linked. Every dot sits beside what it links to, parts of each region are named ("Born in Paris"), and lines show how they hang together as you zoom in.
- "And 834 more" opens: every long list shows up to 5,040 names, on pages, on atlas pages and on the Map.
- Every entry says at the top how many more links open data holds, and takes you to them.
- 55 new stories, from who sold what to whom before the war to what really split Standard Oil, each one written from saved sources and checked by a separate reader.
- Connect no longer finds a way through one writer's view of who influenced whom, or through a tale the record does not support.

## 0.4.3 (2026-11-01)

- The Earth came up flat and empty, with no dots, for anyone who had visited before. It now draws the globe and every dot each time.
- The timeline now works on every dot: pick an era and the million fade to what was around then, on the Map and on the Earth.
- Click a dot and see the stories it is in, or the ones it reaches through a link. Clicking a hand-written dot now shows its links, as an imported dot does.
- Three new stories: "The Sealed Train" on how Lenin crossed wartime Germany, "Ninety-Five Theses" on Luther and the printing press, and "The Oil Weapon" on the 1973 embargo. Each joins stories that stood apart.
- 29 entries pointed at the wrong thing in Wikidata, so NASA's page linked to a song. Fixed.
- 44 more sourced links, found by a new scan of what the record is missing and checked by hand against a source.

## 0.4.2 (2026-11-01)

- Connect any two: pick any two of a million things and see the way between them, stop by stop and on the Map.
- The Map shows how things connect: a web of links at every zoom, and click a dot to draw lines to everything it links to.
- Search jumps to any dot on the Map.
- The Earth now shows 216,000 places, and fills in as you zoom. Zoom in on Switzerland and it is full.
- A new look: black on white, with colour only on the dots, and every page lined up with the header. Nothing overlaps on Home or the Map.
- Two new stories: "The Trust" on the rise and breakup of Standard Oil, and "Thirty-Seven Days" on how two shots in Sarajevo became a war across Europe.
- On a phone, slide a finger along a story's scene dots to pick a scene.

## 0.4.1 (2026-11-01)

- A million dots. The best-known people, places, works and events in the world are now on the Map, each with its own page, drawn from open data and marked as such. What we wrote by hand stays apart and keeps its proof.
- The Map is one round world, with every region named, and it stays smooth at a million dots: it only draws what is in view.
- Search finds all of them.
- Four new stories: "We Choose the Moon", "The Plumbers" on Watergate, "The Most Terrible Bomb" on the road to Hiroshima and Nagasaki, and "The Crash of 2008".
- Pictures: story scenes and entries now show freely licensed photos, each credited.
- A new look: real typefaces, colour that means something, every story in its own ink, and the real map of links on the home page.
- Stories now read on white, whatever your system's dark mode says.
- Dots on the Map, in stories and in the explorer take the colour of what they are.
- A dark mode in deep indigo instead of black.

## 0.4.0 (2026-11-01)

- A new story, "The Airfield and the Think Tank": one airfield in Santa Monica, from the first flight around the world to a satellite and a network on paper and the Pentagon Papers, with the ARPANET's builders and Paul Baran each in their own words.
- About 30 new entries around it, from the DC-3 to the prisoner's dilemma.
- Every entry now lists the written stories it appears in, so a reader who lands on a page can find the story around it.
- Every entry and written story now ends with a way to correct it: tell us with a source, or edit the page.

## 0.3.0 (2026-11-01)

- The Map's Earth shows the world of the era you pick: every polity of that year, from 3000 BCE to 1990, in place of today's borders.
- The era slider has stops that fit history: closer together as it comes closer.
- The map of links now fills the page. A small group of entries had drifted far away and pushed everything else into a corner.

## 0.2.2 (2026-10-31)

- The site now goes by Connect the Dots everywhere, including link previews and the share image.
- The home page's title and description say what the site is.
- The footer of every page says who makes it, with a link to Prototype.

## 0.2.1 (2026-10-31)

- Stories on phones: a scene with a long quote or a big number no longer hides its graph under the card.
- On short phones, the card over a story keeps to a few lines, so the map or graph stays in view.

## 0.2.0 (2026-10-31)

- Two new stories. "The Week the Buying Stopped" follows GameStop in January 2021 through each party's own records. "From Clay to Print" follows writing from accounts in Uruk to a printed Bible in Mainz, with who wrote the Torah as each side tells it.
- Thirteen Days now shows the world of 1962, and Turkey's border with the Soviet Union.
- Big numbers in stories, each with its proof beside it.
- Maps of the distant past no longer show today's borders, roads and names.
- Every written story has a cover drawn from its own map, and ends with where to go next.

## 0.1.4 (2026-10-31)

## 0.1.3 (2026-10-30)

- A link to any page now shows a preview image when it is shared, with that page's own title and description.

## 0.1.2 (2026-10-30)

## 0.1.1 (2026-10-30)

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-30)

The first version online, at [dots.joinprototype.com](https://dots.joinprototype.com). Search engines are asked not to list it yet.

- 378 entries, 742 sourced links and 540 sources, each link showing its proof
- Ask: routes between two things, and answers to "who is behind", "who benefited" and "why did it happen"
- Four illustrated stories that play by scrolling or by Listen, with maps that show the borders of their date
- The explorer and the Map, from one dot to the whole Earth
- Made for phones as well as laptops
- Open: the code and data are public, and [LICENSING.md](LICENSING.md) says on what terms
