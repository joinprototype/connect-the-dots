# Changelog

What changed in each version of the site. [docs/deploy.md](docs/deploy.md#versions) says when the number goes up.

## 0.5.17 (2026-11-12)

- The home page names today's puzzle under the picture of the Map, "From United Arab Emirates to Grover Cleveland", with how few steps it takes, or that you solved it today. "More ways in" lists the puzzle too.

## 0.5.16 (2026-11-12)

- Each story's cover is drawn from its own web: the people, bodies, events and works its scenes bring in, each in its kind's colour, and the links the record holds between them, with the best-connected one in black. Before, most covers were a few dots on a line or a made-up star.
- A story's card says how many scenes it has and the years it spans, where it said "Story".

## 0.5.15 (2026-11-12)

- On a phone, the Map no longer jumps away after a two-finger zoom: lifting one finger used to move the map by everything that finger had moved during the zoom.
- Visits are counted with Cloudflare's Web Analytics, which sets no cookie and keeps no visitor id; before, the site's own security policy blocked it.

## 0.5.14 (2026-11-12)

- A new story, Midnight, 1947, follows the partition of British India: from Jinnah's letter of February 1946 and the Cabinet Mission's plan to Mountbatten's date, Radcliffe's five weeks and the awards published after independence, with four survivors in their own words and the counts of the displaced and the dead, each with who made it.
- A new story, How America Became a Superpower, goes from the war with Spain in 1898 to Marshall's offer in 1947: the Philippine war and its dead by each count, Panama, Wilson's war, the war debts, Lend-Lease, Bretton Woods, and the governments that bargained or refused.
- The Trust goes from 18 scenes to 22: the railroad deals after the South Improvement Company, the Supreme Court's 1911 opinion in its own words, what became of the separated companies, and Rockefeller's and Flagler's own defences.
- The daily puzzle no longer offers names already on your way and shows each name once; "Back a step" undoes a step, and after each step the page says how far the end is.
- Nine new links from the nightly leads run, among them the headquarters of NASA, the EPA and the CFTC and Arthur Levitt at Goldman Sachs, and the Internet Archive's copy for 771 more sources.

## 0.5.13 (2026-11-06)

- The Week the Buying Stopped goes from 16 scenes to 21: what Interactive Brokers, Webull and its clearing firm Apex decided that week and why, in their own words; Robinhood's $3.4 billion raise; FINRA's 2021 fine and what it was for, which was not the January restrictions; the Massachusetts case; GameStop's own share sales of 2021; and Keith Gill's return in 2024.
- The Internet Archive's copy for 170 more sources.

## 0.5.12 (2026-11-05)

- Every story but the four about Jeffrey Epstein is retold through the people who decided: in each scene a king, an executive, a judge, a soldier or a family at a moment, what they faced and chose, their reasons in their own words where the record has them, and, where it has both, what they said in public beside what they said in private.
- A new story, Liberty and the Terror, follows the French Revolution from Calonne's tax plan in 1787 to Bonaparte's coup in 1799, with the votes on the king and the Terror's dead by each historian's count.
- One Land names the claim that the Rothschilds created Israel as antisemitic, as the fact-check it cites does.
- Five new links from the nightly leads run, and the Internet Archive's copy for 147 more sources.

## 0.5.11 (2026-11-04)

- A new story, Why Empires Fall, sets side by side what the record's historians and witnesses say ended Rome's west, the Ottomans, the Mongol Empire and the Yuan, and the Soviet Union: money, armies, outside pressure, disease, succession, reform and transformation, each in its holder's name, with no rule of its own.
- A source with no saved copy now gets the Internet Archive's copy closest to the day it was read, checked a seventh of the sources a night.

## 0.5.10 (2026-11-04)

- Five new stories: Why Rome Fell, the evidence and then each historian's explanation in their own name; Six Hundred Years, the Ottomans from Osman to the end of the caliphate on 3 March 1924; From Four Ships to Portsmouth, Japan from 1853 to 1911, with Taiwan's and Korea's side as far as the record read allows; Reform and Opening, China from 1978, with whose poverty line and whose count is whose; and The House of Wisdom, Baghdad's learning and what Europe took from it, legend set apart on both sides.
- Behind the Success Stories goes from 10 scenes to 23: Traf-O-Data, the MITS deal, IBM and Digital Research with the flying story told as a story, the 1986 share sale, the Amazon prospectus, Facemash and the lawsuits.
- A story's graph now shows both ends of every link a scene reveals, named, where before one end could sit out of view.
- A link's proof reads the same on a story, the map, Ask and the puzzle: one side's account names whose it is, and a source's saved copy is always "Archived copy".
- On a phone, a place's name on a story's map no longer sits under the date in the corner.

## 0.5.9 (2026-11-04)

- Three new stories: The Mongol Empire, from Temüjin to the fall of the Yuan in 1368, every death toll the chronicler's own; Built to Last, what Kublai's capital and the Mughals built, what they took from others, and what later builders took from them; and How Europe Pulled Ahead, from the "Dark Ages" label to 1914, with income estimates, the Black Death, silver, coal, the Opium War, and eight historians' explanations, none of them the narrator's.
- Five stories go deeper. The Rise and Fall of the Soviet Union now spends most of its 20 scenes on why the Union ended: grain, oil, the treasury, the arms race, Chernobyl, the republics, the coup and Ukraine's vote, each explanation in its holder's name. Thirteen Days runs 20 scenes, with the U-2 photographs, submarine B-59 and the secret deal over the missiles in Turkey. We Choose the Moon adds Sputnik, Gagarin, the cost counted two ways and who stopped the landings. The Plumbers adds the 21 March tape, the end of the Ellsberg case, the fight over the tapes and the rules Congress wrote after. From Clay to Print adds the Rosetta Stone, China's oracle bones, the Maya script, the Library of Alexandria and its legend, paper's road west, and type in China and Korea before Gutenberg.
- The stories a story suggests next share truer names: an old rule had dropped any entry whose name held a word like "States" or "Committee".
- The puzzle's trail no longer breaks beside an open proof.

## 0.5.8 (2026-11-04)

- The Sealed Train says what Berlin's million roubles for Parvus is in today's money: about $10 million at 2026 prices, or nearly half a tonne of gold, with the sums and their sources at the end of the story.

## 0.5.7 (2026-11-04)

- A story set in the past no longer shows a city or a company as it looks today: no 2009 skyline beside 1917, no modern headquarters beside 1830. Those scenes show a person or a picture of the time, or the map alone.
- One more sourced link from the nightly loop: Texaco, part of Chevron since their 2001 merger.

## 0.5.6 (2026-11-04)

- A puzzle a day: two names, and you step from one to the other by links on the record, each with its proof, in as few steps as you can. Share your result as a line of dots that gives nothing away.
- Missing links: what open data says and the record does not hold yet, each a question. Know a source? Send it, and once it is checked it goes on the record.
- Ten more sourced links, the first found by a cheap model and checked by a careful one: OPEC's and the IEA's members, Kennedy Space Center in NASA, Google in Alphabet, Felipe VI as King of Spain and John McCone at the Atomic Energy Commission.

## 0.5.5 (2026-11-03)

- The Map's Earth names what is where from the first view, more as you zoom in, and its land is a light grey everywhere, Europe included.
- The home page no longer repeats the header's links.
- A stake's bar on an entry page is now its share of the whole: Elon Musk's 19.8% of Tesla no longer fills the bar as if it were all of it.
- Time axes no longer cut their last year short.
- Ask reads each link as a sentence: "Moses, regarded as a prophet in Christianity", where it used to drop the comma. Names in its heading are dotted under in their colour, as in stories.

## 0.5.4 (2026-11-03)

- Stories is the shelf: every story's cover, to pick one and read it. The islands they make are on the Map.

## 0.5.3 (2026-11-03)

- The old map's code is gone: about 5,800 lines and five packages (deck.gl, MapLibre), and about 3,200 files the site no longer needs. The home page's map picture is drawn from the islands, and pages may no longer fetch anything from another site.

## 0.5.2 (2026-11-03)

- The Map is one picture of everything read: every story an island, every entry a dot. Pick a dot and its links draw while the rest steps back, with each link in words beside it.
- "On Earth" moves whatever has a place onto the world: places, events, and companies where they are headquartered. People are never placed.
- A year typed in the box opens Ask's answer for that year.

## 0.5.1 (2026-11-03)

- One look on every page: black on white, whatever the system's dark mode says. Stories open on their title on white, covers and the footer are white, and nothing large is black any more.
- The header stays where it is on the Map, as on every other page.
- The Map draws only what has been read: 3,453 entries and their links, each with its proof. Open data, which nobody here has read, leaves it; it stays in search and on its own pages.

## 0.5.0 (2026-11-03)

- One view for each thing you do. Every story reads on Read, at its own address, over the world in dots; so do the stories drawn for a name or a question. Ask answers any two names. Explore and Connect are gone, and their old addresses take you to the same names on Stories, Ask or their pages.
- Ask shows what you can type: a name, two names, a year or a question, each with an example to tap.
- The maps beside a story come no nearer than a country, so the land still reads, and they name only the story's own places.
- The box no longer reads a word for a people as a name: "black people" is not Leon Black.

## 0.4.10 (2026-11-03)

- Read's pictures, and Ask's world, show their names again: since 0.4.9 each name sat under a black bar, because the look of the dots was written to a file no page loaded. The build now stops if that happens again.

## 0.4.9 (2026-11-03)

- Ask: one big box at /ask/. Type a name, two names, a year or a question, and the page becomes the answer, with its proof and its picture. It opens already asking how two things connect.
- Stories is one place you move on: every story an island, named at rest. Zoom in and its dots are named; click one and glide to it, with its words, links and proof beside it, and find the way from it to anything else. Only what a story is about lies on its island; whoever it shares or only mentions floats in the water nearby.
- Read: a story over the world drawn in dots, at /read/. Any name opens with its link and proof, and at the end the next story offers itself, with what it shares.

## 0.4.8 (2026-11-03)

- The Map, Stories and Explore are back in the header on every page, beside the box. Home shows the Map, and lists every way in.
- A story now ends on the stories that share the rarest names with it, each saying what it shares.

## 0.4.7 (2026-11-02)
